# Cross-border data regimes compared — GDPR Chapter V vs China's 2024 provisions

English · [中文](03-cross-border-data-zh.md)

> One question: **when may personal data leave the country?**
> Short answer: **the EU decides at the country level and then lets data move; China decides at the flow level and only opens a route once you cross a numeric threshold.**
>
> Evidence grades (defined in [`00-overview.md`](00-overview.md)): **A** primary text, checked live; **B** other official source or a direct reading of the primary text; **C** our own comparison or inference, or not yet checked.

## 1. The table

| Dimension | EU | China | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instrument | GDPR Chapter V (Articles 44–49) | PIPL Article 38 + the 2024 Provisions (CAC Order No. 16) | A |
| Routes | adequacy decision; appropriate safeguards (SCCs, BCRs, codes, certification); narrow derogations | security assessment; filed standard contract; certification | A |
| Unit of decision | **country-level** (adequacy) or **contract-level** (SCCs) | **flow-level**, by exporter and by volume | B |
| Default posture | transfer permitted once a route applies | transfer permitted only through a specific route, and only above the exemption line | B |
| Thresholds | none | **non-CIIO only:** under 100,000 individuals' PI with no sensitive PI and no important data → nothing to do; 100,000–1,000,000 non-sensitive, or under 10,000 sensitive → contract or certification; 1,000,000+ non-sensitive, 10,000+ sensitive, or any important data → security assessment. Sensitive PI has **no** small-volume exemption | A |
| Who you are | no equivalent trigger | **CIIO** status pulls any transfer of personal information or important data into security assessment, whatever the volume (Art. 7(1)) | A |
| Multi-national group | BCRs (a formal approval process) | the 2024 Provisions exempt certain HR-management transfers | A |
| Sector-specific trigger | none in the GDPR itself | **important data** — but only data a department or region has told you, or publicly listed, as important (Art. 2) | A |
| Filing | none for SCCs; BCRs need approval | standard contracts must be filed with the provincial CAC | A |
| Exemptions | Article 49 derogations, read narrowly | 2024 Provisions list trade, academic cooperation, emergency, HR and small-volume exemptions (Arts. 3–6) | A |
| Result validity | adequacy reviewed periodically | security assessment result valid 3 years, extendable once by another 3 (Art. 9) | A |
| Penalties | GDPR Article 83 (up to 4% of worldwide turnover) | PIPL Article 66 (up to RMB 50m or 5% of prior-year turnover) | A |

## 2. Five observations

1. **Trust abroad vs permission for the flow.** The EU asks "is this country adequate?" and then data moves. China asks "how much is leaving from this exporter?" and prescribes an act. The first is faster once granted; the second is more predictable per flow. — **C**
2. **Numbers make China's regime legible.** Because the triggers are volume-based, a compliance team can compute whether it needs anything — which is exactly why the 2024 relaxation did so much for multinationals. — **C**
3. **Both treat HR data as the awkward case.** The EU route is BCRs; China wrote an exemption. Same problem, opposite fixes. — **C**
4. **Important data and CIIO have no EU analogue.** In China a flow can be pulled into security assessment because of *what* the data is or *who* the exporter is, not just how much. — **A**
5. **The practical consequence:** an EU-only playbook does not satisfy China. If you operate in both, you need adequacy plus SCCs for the EU side, and separate volume maths plus a filing or assessment for the Chinese side. — **C**

## 3. What it means for a company in both markets

- Keep two transfer registers, not one: one keyed by destination country, one keyed by annual volume per exporter. — **C**
- Do not assume the standard contract is portable — China's must be filed, the EU's are pre-approved clauses. — **A**
- Watch the free-trade-zone negative lists in China; they create genuinely different rules inside one country. — **C**
- Treat the small-volume exemption as **open to non-sensitive personal information only**: one sensitive record takes you out of it. — **A**

## 4. Limits and unverified ⚠

- The Chinese thresholds have been read article by article against the official text — Articles 5, 7 and 8 — on 2026-10-07. — **A**
- The certification measure, CAC and SAMR Order No. 20, was published 2025-10-14 and takes effect only on **2026-01-01**. Until then the certification route rests on the 2024 Provisions and the pre-existing certification rules, not on that order. — **A**
- Sectoral "important data" catalogues are still issued piecemeal. — **B**
- Adequacy decisions and the EU–US Data Privacy Framework's status change over time. — **C**
