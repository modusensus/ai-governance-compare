# Liability and pending law compared — EU vs China

> One question: **if AI causes harm, who pays — and what is still coming?**
> Short answer: **neither side has a dedicated AI civil-liability law.** The EU deliberately dropped one and modernised product liability instead; China has comprehensive AI legislation on the 2026 State Council plan, but no text yet.
>
> Evidence grades (defined in [`00-overview.md`](00-overview.md)): **A** primary text, checked live; **B** other official source or a direct reading of the primary text; **C** our own comparison or inference, or not yet checked.

## 1. The table

| Dimension | EU | China | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dedicated AI liability instrument | none — AI Liability Directive **withdrawn 11 February 2025** | none | A |
| What fills the gap | revised **Product Liability Directive (EU) 2024/2853** | Civil Code: tort liability + product liability | A |
| Timing | in force 2024-12-08; national transposition by **2026-12-09** | already applicable | B |
| Software and AI as "products" | yes — PLD covers software and AI systems | unsettled whether an AI-enabled service is a "product" ⚠ | B |
| Burden of proof | disclosure duty + **rebuttable presumption** for complex products | ordinary civil burden; no AI-specific rule | A |
| Administrative liability | AI Act, DSA, DMA, GDPR penalties | Generative AI Interim Measures, Deep Synthesis Provisions, Algorithmic Recommendation Provisions, PIPL, DSL, CSL — but neither AI measure **sets a fine amount of its own**: Art 21 of the generative measures enumerates the general laws and adds its own ladder ending in suspension, while Art 22 of the deep-synthesis provisions refers generally and aggravates where serious consequences result | A |
| Ethics gate | risk management under the AI Act | Measures for Ethical Review of Science and Technology (trial), effective 2023-12-01 | A |
| Pending AI law | none for liability; AI Act implementation continues | **comprehensive AI legislation directed by the State Council 2026 plan** (no draft yet) | B |

## 2. Five observations

1. **The EU chose "no AI-specific liability".** Rather than adopt the AI Liability Directive, it withdrew the proposal and updated product liability — a bet that general law plus a softer evidential burden is enough. — **C**
2. **China is heading the other way, upwards.** Its plan language escalated from study to **comprehensive legislation**, and now names the AI stack — data, computing power, algorithms, IP, network and supply-chain security — plus key application scenarios. — **B**
3. **Today, China's liability is administrative-first.** Regulators act through filing, security assessment, warnings, suspension of information updates and fines that are modest under the AI-specific rules; private claims rely on the Civil Code. The ceiling is higher outside the AI-specific rules: since 1 January 2026 the amended Cybersecurity Law reaches **RMB 10 million** for the most serious network-security failures, with up to RMB 1 million on responsible individuals. — **A**
4. **After December 2026, the EU shifts toward product-law-first.** A defective AI-enabled product becomes pursuable with a presumption in the claimant's favour — a meaningful change for software vendors. — **B**
5. **Two acknowledged gaps line up.** The EU's withdrawn directive targeted pure economic loss and non-product AI harm; China has no burden-of-proof rule for AI damage either. The instruments differ; the hole is similar. — **C**

## 3. What it means in practice

- If you ship AI-enabled products into the EU, prepare for **December 2026** transposition: evidence retention and technical documentation become litigation-relevant. — **B**
- In China, compliance work currently centres on **administrative filings and assessments**, not on defending civil claims — but a comprehensive law would likely add liability allocation. — **C**
- Watching brief: the first Chinese AI law draft, and the first PLD cases after transposition. Both will move this comparison. — **C**

## 4. Limits and unverified ⚠

- The PLD publication date and transposition wording should be confirmed against the Official Journal. — **B** ⚠
- The penalty articles are carded, and they differ. **Article 21** of the [Generative AI Interim Measures](../cn/generative-ai-services-measures.md) enumerates the Cybersecurity Law, Data Security Law, Personal Information Protection Law and Science and Technology Progress Law, and where those are silent provides warning, circulated criticism and an order to correct — suspension of the relevant service on refusal or serious cases. **Article 22** of the [Deep Synthesis Provisions](../cn/deep-synthesis-provisions.md) refers generally to the relevant laws and administrative regulations, with heavier punishment where serious consequences result, and no fallback. Neither states an amount, so the heavy numbers belong to the general laws, not to the AI rules. — **A**
- The Chinese AI law's scope and timing are speculative until a draft is published. — **C** ⚠
- Whether Chinese courts treat AI-enabled services as "products" is not settled by statute. — **C** ⚠
