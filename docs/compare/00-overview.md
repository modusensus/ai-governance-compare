# EU vs China — AI and data law, an overview

English · [中文](00-overview-zh.md)

> How to read: start with section 1 for the picture and the one-line conclusion, then go as deep as you need. Items marked ⚠ could not be verified against the primary text — treat the official wording as authoritative.
>
> **Evidence grades, used in every comparison file.** **A** = the primary official text — statute, regulation, mandatory standard — checked live against the link in the file's `source`. **B** = another official source (regulator guidance, an official press release, Commission pages), or a direct mechanical reading of the primary text. **C** = this repository's own comparison or inference, or a claim not yet traced back to the primary text; a C never supports a conclusion on its own, and an unchecked one keeps the ⚠. ## 0. The difference in one line

**The EU passes one big statute (the AI Act), sorts everything by risk, and governs every sector horizontally.**
**China issues one rule per problem area (algorithms, deep synthesis, generative AI …), governs whatever surfaces first, and optimises for what works in practice.**

Think of it this way: the EU draws a city master plan and then builds to it; China fixes whichever road is jammed, and the network grows organically.

## 1. The table

| Dimension | EU | China | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Legislative style | one horizontal statute + risk tiers | sector-specific rules, patched piece by piece | C |
| Core AI law | AI Act (2024/1689; in force 2024-08-01) | AI Law still in the legislative plan; governed meanwhile by the Generative AI Interim Measures and similar | A |
| Data protection | GDPR (2016/679; applicable 2018-05-25) | Personal Information Protection Law (effective 2021-11-01) | A |
| Cross-border data | adequacy decisions + SCCs + BCRs | security assessment / standard contract / certification (three routes; relaxed in 2024) | A |
| Content and labelling | AI Act transparency duties + DSA platform duties | Deep Synthesis Provisions (2023-01-10) + Labelling Measures (2025-09-01) + Anthropomorphic Interaction Services Measures (2026-07-15) | A |
| Platforms | DSA + DMA (gatekeepers) | Algorithmic Recommendation Provisions (2022-03-01) + platform responsibility; app stores verify assessment and filing before listing (2026) | A |
| Enforcement | GDPR up to 4% of worldwide turnover; AI Act up to 7% / EUR 35m | rectification orders, warnings, takedowns, service suspension; lower statutory fines | A |
| Extraterritorial reach | GDPR Article 3; fines have landed on non-EU firms | PIPL Article 3 also claims extraterritorial effect; enforcement is more case-by-case | B |
| Orientation | fundamental rights first (privacy, non-discrimination) | development and security in tandem (holistic national security) | C |

## 2. The EU's ten main tracks

1. **AI Act (2024/1689)** — the constitution for AI. Four risk tiers: unacceptable (banned) / high (strict) / limited (transparency only) / minimal (barely regulated). — **A**
   - Staged application (Article 113, as amended in 2026 by the Digital Omnibus on AI): prohibitions from 2025-02-02; GPAI duties, governance and penalties from 2025-08-02; Article 50 transparency from 2026-08-02; Annex III high-risk from **2027-12-02**; Annex I high-risk from **2028-08-02**. — **A**
2. **GDPR (2016/679)** — personal data; transfers under Articles 44–49. — **A**
3. **Data Governance Act (2022/868)** — safe re-use of public-sector data. — **A**
4. **Data Act (2023/2854)** — who may use data generated by connected devices. — **A**
5. **Digital Services Act (2022/2065)** — platform duties on illegal content, transparency, algorithmic explanation. — **A**
6. **Digital Markets Act (2022/1925)** — gatekeeper platforms and contestability. — **A**
7. **ePrivacy Directive (2002/58/EC)** — cookies and electronic communications privacy. Listed for completeness; no card yet. — **A**
8. **AI Liability Directive** — 2022 proposal, **withdrawn** by the Commission in its 2025 Work Programme (adopted 2025-02-11). No AI-specific civil liability instrument is planned; the gap it targeted is now handled by the Product Liability Directive below. — **B**
9. **New Product Liability Directive (2024/2853)** — brings software and AI into product liability; in force since 2024-12-08, and it binds products placed on the market from **2026-12-09** (the Member State transposition deadline). — **A**
10. **Cyber Resilience Act (2024/2847) + NIS2 (2022/2555)** — security baselines for digital products and critical infrastructure. — **A**

> Three layers stack: an AI Act for AI, a GDPR for data, DSA/DMA for platforms.

## 3. China's nine main tracks

1. **Cybersecurity Law (effective 2017-06-01; amended 2025-10-28, in force 2026-01-01)** — network operations, critical information infrastructure. The amendment adds an AI article (new Article 20) and sharply raises penalties, up to RMB 10 million. — **A**
2. **Data Security Law (effective 2021-09-01)** — data classification, important data, national security. — **A**
3. **Personal Information Protection Law (effective 2021-11-01)** — the domestic GDPR, with cross-border rules in Articles 38–43. — **A**
4. **Algorithmic Recommendation Provisions (effective 2022-03-01)** — recommendation algorithms must be filed, switchable off, and not induce addiction. — **A**
5. **Deep Synthesis Provisions (effective 2023-01-10)** — face swaps and synthetic speech must be labelled and must not be used to spread disinformation. — **A**
6. **Interim Measures for Generative AI Services (effective 2023-08-15)** — public generative AI services need filing, security assessment and content compliance. — **A**
7. **Labelling Measures for AI-Generated Synthetic Content (effective 2025-09-01)** — explicit and implicit labels for AI-generated text, images, audio, video and virtual scenes. — **A**
8. **Provisions on Promoting and Regulating Cross-Border Data Flows (effective 2024-03-22)** — lowers thresholds, defines exemptions, works alongside the 2022 Security Assessment Measures. — **A**
   - Also **Measures for Ethical Review of Science and Technology (trial)** (effective 2023-12-01) ⚠ as an ethics backstop. — **C**
   - **AI Law**: repeatedly in the State Council legislative plan; not yet enacted ⚠. — **B**
9. **Anthropomorphic Interaction Services Measures (Order No. 21, effective 2026-07-15)** — the first rule aimed at AI companions rather than AI content: no induced emotional dependency or manipulation, no virtual relatives or virtual partners for minors, a usage reminder every two hours of continuous use, interaction data the user can copy or delete, and a bar on training on users' sensitive interaction data without separate consent. — **A**

> Three pillars — Cybersecurity Law, Data Security Law, PIPL — with the AI layer filled in by departmental rules.

## 4. Eight dimensions

### 4.1 What is regulated — **A**
- The EU regulates the **AI system** by risk tier, with duties on providers and deployers.
- China regulates the **service provider**: whoever offers algorithmic, synthesis or generative services to the public must file and pass security assessment.

### 4.2 Generative AI — **A**
- EU: inside the AI Act's GPAI chapter — model cards, training-data summaries, copyright policy, adversarial testing for systemic risk.
- China: a dedicated interim measure plus the labelling measures — filing, content safety, labelling.

### 4.3 Cross-border data (where firms get stuck) — **A**
- EU: check for an adequacy decision first; otherwise SCCs or BCRs.
- China: security assessment, standard contract or certification, with exemptions widened in 2024.

### 4.4 Penalties — **A**
- The EU fines hard (GDPR 4%; AI Act up to 7%) and has done so.
- China leans on rectification, warnings, takedowns and suspension; statutory fines are lower, but a takedown hurts a platform more than a fine. *(The second half of this sentence is our judgement.)* — **B**

### 4.5 Platforms — **A**
- The EU adds duties by size and demands algorithmic explanation.
- China applies platform responsibility plus algorithm filing, aimed at content and order.

### 4.6 The language of rights — **C** *(our framing, not a legal claim)*
- The EU speaks in rights: data-subject rights, fundamental rights.
- China speaks in security, development and order; individual rights sit inside PIPL, but the frame serves national security.

### 4.7 Who enforces — **A**
- EU: national data protection authorities plus the AI Office, coordinated through the consistency mechanism.
- China: CAC leads, with industry, public security and market regulators alongside.

### 4.8 AI-generated intimate content about real people — **A**
- EU: the AI Act requires disclosure of AI generation; the GDPR covers biometric and image-related data; the DSA obliges platforms to act on illegal content.
- China: the Deep Synthesis Provisions and Labelling Measures prohibit using synthesis to spread disinformation or infringe rights, and require platforms to label and act.
- **Shared blind spot:** once content is posted to a platform abroad, neither regime can reach it — the governance gap. — **C**

## 5. Why cross-border governance is the hardest part

In one line: **law is national, the internet is not.**

- One country finds content unlawful; a platform in another can ignore it absent mutual legal assistance or voluntary cooperation. — **C**
- Data flows: the EU uses adequacy decisions; China uses assessment / contract / certification. The two systems do not recognise each other. — **A** *(first sentence)* / **C** *(second)*
- AI-generated content: the EU emphasises transparency, China emphasises labelling and content safety — similar direction, **non-overlapping jurisdiction**. — **C**

Product-level mechanisms that put authorisation, revocation, identity and audit at the entry point can work around the absence of legislation — but ask where the data sits, whose law governs it, and who gets to see the audit.

## 6. Three entry points for research

1. **Compare labelling regimes** — China's September 2025 measures vs the EU's transparency duty: which is finer, which is actually enforceable? — **C**
2. **The cross-border enforcement gap** — using AI-generated intimate videos of real people as the case: both regimes reach the conduct on paper, neither reaches the platform abroad. — **C**
3. **Where expert identity lands** — comparative work like this converts naturally into assessment reports and standards input. — **C**

## 7. Limits and unverified ⚠

- Stage dates for the AI Act reflect Article 113 as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (the Digital Omnibus on AI); check the consolidated text before relying on them. — **A**
- The AI Law's progress needs checking against the latest legislative plan. The Cybersecurity Law amendment is confirmed (adopted 2025-10-28, in force 2026-01-01). — **B**
- Penalty caps (GDPR 4%, AI Act 7%) are statutory maxima; actual fines are far lower. — **A**
- The ePrivacy Directive (2002/58/EC) is listed as a track but has no card yet, so it is not compared anywhere in this repository. — **A**
