# Ownership of AI-generated content compared — EU vs China

English · [中文](07-ai-output-ownership-zh.md)

> One question: **when a model generates an image, does either regime say who owns it?**
>
> Short answer: **no text secured in this repository answers that, and neither instrument was written to.** Both sides regulate the **input** — the EU requires a general-purpose model provider to hold a copyright-compliance policy, China requires training data of lawful origin and no infringement of intellectual property others enjoy — and both require the **output** to be disclosed as generated. A label answers *"was this generated?"*; it does not answer *"who owns it?"*. Ownership is a copyright-law question, and this repository has carded neither side's copyright statute.
>
> Evidence grades (defined in [`00-overview.md`](00-overview.md)): **A** primary text, checked live; **B** other official source or a direct reading of the primary text; **C** our own comparison or inference, or not yet checked.

## 2. What each side actually says

### EU — one operative copyright clause, and it is about training

- **Article 53(1)(c)** obliges providers of general-purpose AI models to put in place a copyright-compliance policy; the full sentence is in the quote section. — **A**
- **Recital 105** sets out why: large generative models require access to vast amounts of text, images, videos and other data, text and data mining may be used on content *which may be protected by copyright and related rights*, and *any use of copyright protected content requires the authorisation of the rightsholder concerned unless relevant copyright exceptions and limitations apply*. — **A**
- **Recital 106** makes the policy duty jurisdiction-independent: the provider must comply *regardless of the jurisdiction in which the copyright-relevant acts underpinning the training … take place*. — **A**
- **Recital 107** frames the training-data summary as a transparency device so that parties with legitimate interests, *including copyright holders*, can exercise and enforce their rights under Union law. — **A**
- **Recital 108** draws the boundary around the Act: monitoring does not involve a work-by-work assessment of training data in terms of copyright compliance, and *this Regulation does not affect the enforcement of copyright rules as provided for under Union law*. — **A**
- **Article 50(2)** is the output-side duty, and it is a marking duty, not a rights rule: the outputs of a generative system *shall be marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated*. The transparency card is at [`ai-act-transparency.md`](../eu/ai-act-transparency.md). — **A**
- **Reading of the whole act:** `copyright` occurs on eight lines of the snapshot — Recitals 104–108, Article 53(1)(c), and the citation list that names Directive (EU) 2019/790 — and no operative provision assigns a right in what a model generates, nor defines who the author of an output is. — **B** *(direct mechanical reading of the primary text)*

### China — conduct duties on the service provider, not an allocation of rights

- **Article 4(3)** of the Generative AI Interim Measures puts respect for intellectual property and business ethics among the conduct rules binding providers and users of generative AI services. — **A**
- **Article 7(2)** is a training-data rule: where intellectual property is involved, do not infringe the intellectual property others enjoy by law — a prohibition on infringement, not a grant or an allocation of rights. — **A**
- **Article 12** carries the output-side duty across to the labelling rules: the provider must label generated images, video and similar content as required by the Deep Synthesis Provisions. — **A**
- The **Labelling Measures** (effective 2025-09-01) define generated synthetic content as text, images, audio, video and virtual scenes, define explicit and implicit labels (Article 3), require a visible label at an appropriate place **on the image** (Article 4(3)), and require implicit marking in the file's metadata (Article 5). — **A**
- **Reading of the secured texts:** they regulate how the service is built and how its output is marked; none of them says who holds a right in the generated image. — **B**

### The one place the ownership question is on a plan

- The **State Council 2026 Legislative Work Plan** (issued 2026-05-11) directs comprehensive AI legislation and legislation on AI's shared elements, intellectual property among them. It is a plan item with no text, so no obligation can be cited from it; the card is [`ai-law-and-liability.md`](../cn/ai-law-and-liability.md). — **B**

## 3. Five observations

1. **Both sides legislated the input and the label; neither legislated the title.** The EU clause is a compliance policy for training; the two Chinese clauses are lawful training data and non-infringement. — **C** *(rests on the A readings above)*
2. **The addressee is not the same actor.** The EU duty lands on the provider of a general-purpose AI **model**; the Chinese duties land on the provider of a generative AI **service** offered to the public. The same rightsholder interest is met at a different point in the chain — read them as two provisions, not as parallel ones. — **C**
3. **A label is not a title.** Article 50(2) and the Labelling Measures both answer *"is this generated?"*; neither tells a reader who owns the image. — **C**
4. **Neither instrument defines the author of a generated image, or whether it attracts copyright at all.** That question needs the copyright statutes and the courts, and no text secured here answers it. — **C**
5. **The consequence for readers of this repository:** nothing here may be quoted as saying that an AI-generated image is, or is not, copyrightable, or as naming an owner. The question is open in this repository by design, not by oversight — see section 6. — **C**

## 4. What this comparison does not cover

- **The Copyright Law of the PRC** — no card and no snapshot here. Whether a generated image is a "work" and who counts as its author are decided there and in the courts.
- **Directive (EU) 2019/790** and its Article 4(3) reservation of rights — named by Article 53(1)(c) and by Recital 106, but the repository holds no snapshot of it, so this comparison says nothing about what that reservation requires beyond its name.
- **Case law on either side** — the CJEU's originality standard for photographs and the Chinese judgments that are discussed publicly are outside the set of instruments carded here; this repository does not cite them.
- **The duties that do touch the output** are carded elsewhere and are about disclosure, not ownership: [`ai-act-transparency.md`](../eu/ai-act-transparency.md), [`01-ai-labeling.md`](01-ai-labeling.md), [`ai-generated-content-labeling-measures.md`](../cn/ai-generated-content-labeling-measures.md).
