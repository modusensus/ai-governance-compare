# Platform responsibility compared — DSA/DMA vs China's algorithm provisions

> One question: **what does the law make a platform responsible for?**
> Short answer: **the EU splits the job in two** — content and risk in the DSA, competition in the DMA — and scales duties by size. **China puts both into one procedural device**: register the algorithm, publish how it works, let users switch it off, and answer for what it does.
>
> Evidence grades (defined in [`00-overview.md`](00-overview.md)): **A** primary text, checked live; **B** other official source or a direct reading of the primary text; **C** our own comparison or inference, or not yet checked.

## 1. The table

| Dimension | EU | China | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instrument | DSA (2022/2065) + DMA (2022/1925) | Provisions on the Administration of Algorithmic Recommendations (Order No. 9) | A |
| Applies from | DSA: VLOPs 2023-08-25, all in-scope 2024-02-17. DMA: 2024-03-07 | 2022-03-01 | A |
| Regulatory target | intermediary services and platforms by **size**; separately, **gatekeepers** | providers using **recommendation technology**, by **algorithm type** and public-opinion capacity | A |
| Core device | duties tiered by size; systemic-risk assessment for VLOPs | **filing with the regulator** for services with public-opinion or social-mobilisation capacity, plus a security assessment | A |
| Transparency | recommender-system transparency (DSA Art 27) | tell users recommendation is used; publish principles, purpose and main mechanism (Art 16) | A |
| User control | no direct opt-out duty in the DSA | **must** offer a non-personalised option or an easy switch-off, effective immediately; tags must be selectable and deletable (Art 17) | A |
| Minors | no targeted ads to minors (DSA Art 28) | age-appropriate modes; no algorithmic inducement into addiction (Art 18) | A |
| Competition | the DMA's gatekeeper rules | Art 15: no unreasonable restriction of other providers, no monopoly or unfair competition | A |
| Price discrimination | not a DSA core duty | Art 21: **no unreasonable differential treatment in price or terms** | A |
| Labour | not addressed | Art 20: scheduling algorithms must respect pay, rest and hours | A |
| Enforcement style | independent audits, risk assessments, researcher access; fines up to 6% (DSA), 10%/20% (DMA) | filing number displayed; warnings and corrective orders; refusal or serious cases → suspend information updates + RMB 10,000–100,000 | B |

## 2. Five observations

1. **Filing versus auditing.** China's hinge is a **registration gate**: file within 10 working days, get a number, display it. The EU's hinge is **continuous obligation**: risk assessments, audits, and complaint handling, with no register of algorithms. — **B**
2. **China writes user control into the statute.** The duty to give an off-switch and tag-level control has no direct DSA equivalent — the EU approach is transparency plus risk management, not a mandated opt-out. — **A**
3. **The EU separates content from competition; China merges them.** Article 15 of the Chinese provisions reaches conduct the EU would treat as DMA territory — but through a content-regulation instrument. — **B**
4. **China addresses users the EU largely does not.** Labour scheduling algorithms, elderly-friendly services and price discrimination appear explicitly; the DSA/DMA framework mostly addresses business users and consumers in the abstract. — **A**
5. **Sanction scale differs sharply.** DSA/DMA fines are turnover-based and can reach billions; the Chinese provisions top out at RMB 100,000 plus suspension of information updates — the operational sanction, not the fine, is the deterrent. — **B**

## 3. What it means in practice

- Operating in both markets means two compliance programmes: one built around systemic-risk assessment and audits, one built around filing, disclosure and user-facing controls. — **C**
- The Chinese filing obligation is **fast and procedural** (10 working days) but presupposes a self-assessment report; the EU obligations are slower but continuous. — **B**
- For AI: China's Article 9 ties the algorithm rule directly to content labelling, so an AI feature on a platform can pull in both regimes. — **A**

## 4. Limits and unverified ⚠

- The Chinese provisions are quoted from the official text (CAC); articles above are checked, but implementing guidance on the filing system is not. — **B**
- The DSA/DMA article references and penalty tiers follow the regulations and public guidance; the list of designated VLOPs and gatekeepers changes over time. — **B**
- The DMA's repeat-infringement tier should be confirmed word-by-word against Article 30. — **C** ⚠
