# Data Security Law (DSL)

> 数据安全法. Effective 1 September 2021. The middle pillar: it treats data as a national-security asset and requires classification, not just protection of personal information.

## In one line

Data in China has to be **classified and graded**: the more it matters to national security, the more controls, up to a state security review before it may leave the country.

## Who and what it governs

- Applies to data processing activities in China, covering both electronic and non-electronic data.
- Excludes state secrets, which are handled separately.

## Main obligations

| Area | Substance |
|---|---|
| Classification and grading | Establish a data classification and grading system; apply higher protection to higher grades. |
| Important data | Identify important data, appoint a responsible person, conduct risk assessments, and report risks to the authorities. |
| National security review | Data activities that affect or may affect national security are subject to a security review. |
| Export controls | Export of important data and of data by CII operators is subject to the cross-border regime (PIPL Article 38 and the 2024 provisions). |
| Processing duties | Lawful and proper collection, no unlawful acquisition, and security measures across the lifecycle. |
| Government use | Rules on state authorities collecting and using data, including restrictions on using data for purposes beyond the statutory one. |
| Legal liability | Corrective orders, warnings, fines, suspension of business, revocation of licences, and personal liability. |

## Why it matters

- It is the legal basis for the **important data** concept that drives cross-border assessment obligations.
- It makes data security a matter of **national security**, not only individual rights — the key conceptual contrast with the GDPR.

## Relationship to other instruments

- Sits with the Cybersecurity Law and PIPL as the "three pillars".
- Implemented by the Regulations on Network Data Security Management (effective 2025-01-01).
