# Cybersecurity Law (CSL)

> 网络安全法. Effective 1 June 2017. **Amended on 28 October 2025, in force 1 January 2026**, and re-promulgated with its articles renumbered. The first of China's three data pillars, and the one that created the concepts everything else still uses: critical information infrastructure and network operator duties.

## In one line

Whoever operates a network in China must secure it, register real identities, protect critical information infrastructure — and keep certain data inside the country.

## Key concepts

- **Network operator** — broadly defined, covering anyone who owns, manages or provides network services.
- **Critical information infrastructure (CII)** — systems whose failure could endanger national security, the economy or public interest; CII operators carry heavier duties.
- **Important data** — the concept picked up later by the Data Security Law and the network data regulations.

## Main obligations

| Area | Substance |
|---|---|
| Security management | Internal security systems, responsible staff, technical measures, contingency plans, logs kept for at least six months. |
| Real-name registration | Users must provide real identity information for network access and information publishing. |
| Product and service compliance | Network products and services must meet mandatory national standards and not leave security defects; providers must remediate and report. |
| CII protection | Dedicated security management bodies, regular assessments, and **storage of personal information and important data generated in China within China** — cross-border transfer requires a security assessment. |
| Incident response | Immediate remedial action and reporting to the authorities on an incident. |
| Legal liability | Warnings, fines, suspension of business, revocation of licences, and personal liability for responsible individuals. |

## The 2025 amendment

Adopted by the Standing Committee on **28 October 2025** (14th NPC Standing Committee, 18th session), **in force 1 January 2026**. The law was re-promulgated with its article order adjusted, so the current text — not the 2016 numbering — is the authoritative one.

| Change | Substance |
|---|---|
| **New Article 20 (AI)** | The state supports basic research on AI and key technologies such as algorithms; promotes training-data resources and computing infrastructure; improves AI ethics rules; strengthens risk monitoring, assessment and security regulation; and promotes the healthy development of AI applications. A second paragraph supports innovating network-security management by using AI to raise protection levels. |
| Political principle | A new Article 3 states that cybersecurity work upholds Party leadership, the holistic national-security outlook, and the coordination of development and security. |
| Personal information | Article 40 → 42, with a new paragraph requiring network operators processing personal information to comply with the Civil Code and PIPL as well as this law. |
| **Penalties raised** | Article 59 → 61 now sets three tiers. Ordinary network operators: a warning, or RMB 10,000–50,000, rising to RMB 50,000–500,000 for refusal to correct or harm. CII operators: RMB 50,000–100,000, rising to RMB 100,000–1,000,000. Serious harm (mass data leakage, partial loss of CII function): **RMB 500,000–2,000,000**; especially serious harm (loss of the CII's main function): **RMB 2,000,000–10,000,000**, with personal fines up to RMB 1,000,000 on responsible individuals. |
| New penalty articles | New Article 63 penalises selling or supplying critical network equipment and specialised cybersecurity products without certification or testing; Article 62 → 65 covers certification, testing, risk assessment and the publication of vulnerability information. |
| Cross-statute coordination | Articles 64, 66 and 70 are merged into Article 71, routing personal-information and cross-border violations to the relevant law; a new Article 73 applies the Administrative Penalties Law's rules on lighter, reduced or waived penalties. |
| Overseas reach | Article 75 → 77 keeps the power to sanction overseas bodies, organisations and individuals whose acts endanger Chinese network security, including freezing property. |

## Relationship to other instruments

- Followed by the **Data Security Law** (effective 2021-09-01) and the **Personal Information Protection Law** (effective 2021-11-01).
- Operationalised by the **Regulations on Network Data Security Management** (effective 2025-01-01).
