# Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL)

> 个人信息保护法. Effective 1 November 2021. China's counterpart to the GDPR — similar shape, different foundations.

## In one line

A comprehensive personal-information statute: it lists when processing is lawful, gives individuals a set of rights, and puts cross-border transfers behind three gateways.

## Who and what it governs

- Applies to processing personal information **within China**, and — under Article 3 — also to processing outside China that aims at providing products or services to people in China, or that analyses or assesses behaviour in China.
- "Personal information" means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person, excluding anonymised information.

## Key provisions

| Topic | Provision |
|---|---|
| Lawful bases | **Article 13** — consent; contract; HR management under labour rules; legal duty; public-health emergency; news reporting and public-opinion supervision within the law; other situations in law or regulation. **There is no "legitimate interests" basis.** |
| Sensitive personal information | **Articles 28–29** — biometrics, religious belief, specific identity, medical and health, financial accounts, whereabouts, and the information of minors **under 14**. Requires **separate consent** and a specific-purpose justification. |
| Separate consent | A distinct statutory device, required for cross-border provision, disclosure to another processor, public disclosure, and sensitive data. |
| Individual rights | **Articles 44–50** — knowledge, decision, access and copying, correction, deletion, portability, explanation of automated decisions, and a complaint route. |
| Personal information officer | **Article 52** — processors reaching a volume threshold must designate a person responsible for protection. |
| Impact assessment | **Articles 55–56** — required for sensitive data, automated decision-making, entrusting, disclosure, and cross-border transfers. |
| Breach notice | **Article 57** — notify the authority and affected individuals. |
| Cross-border transfers | **Article 38** — three gateways: security assessment, professional certification, or standard contract; plus the 2024 provisions relaxing thresholds. |
| Penalties | **Article 66** — serious violations: up to **RMB 50 million** or **5% of prior-year turnover**; suspension of service, business suspension and licence revocation; personal liability on responsible individuals. |

## Relationship to other laws

- Sits alongside the Cybersecurity Law and the Data Security Law. The three are usually described as the "three pillars" of Chinese digital regulation.
- Implementing rules for cross-border flow: Provisions on Promoting and Regulating Cross-Border Data Flows (effective 2024-03-22).
