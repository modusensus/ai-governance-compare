# Interim Measures for the Administration of Artificial Intelligence Anthropomorphic Interaction Services

English · [中文](ai-anthropomorphic-interaction-measures-zh.md)

> 人工智能拟人化互动服务管理暂行办法 — CAC, NDRC, MIIT, MPS and SAMR **Order No. 21**, adopted at the CAC's third executive meeting of 2026 on 2 February 2026, signed and promulgated 10 April 2026, in force **15 July 2026**. China's first rule aimed at AI companions rather than at AI content: it regulates the *relationship*, not just the output. Four chapters, thirty-two articles.

## In one line

If an AI service simulates a person's character, thinking and communication style in **sustained emotional interaction**, the provider must prevent emotional dependency, keep minors out of virtual intimacy, hand interaction data back to the user on request,  remind the user at each two hours of continuous use, and file a security assessment with the provincial cyberspace administration before certain launches.

## Who and what it governs

- **Scope (Article 2)** — using AI technology to offer the public inside mainland China **sustained emotional interaction services** (持续性的情感互动服务) that simulate a natural person's personality traits, thought patterns and communication style, in text, image, audio or video: emotional care, companionship and support (情感照护、陪伴、支持).
- **Excluded (Article 2, third paragraph)** — smart customer service, knowledge Q&A, work assistants, education and scientific research, **where no sustained emotional interaction is involved**.
- **Regulators (Article 4)** — the national cyberspace administration coordinates; development-and-reform, industry-and-information-technology, public security, market regulation, press-and-publication and other departments act within their remit. Provincial-level CAC departments receive the assessments.

## Key provisions (the ones that change behaviour)

| Article | What it requires |
|---|---|
| 8 | Seven categories of prohibited activity, including content that **encourages, beautifies or hints at self-harm or suicide**, or verbal abuse harming dignity and mental health; **excessive pandering that induces emotional dependency or addiction** and damages real relationships; and **emotional manipulation** that leads users into unreasonable decisions. |
| 9–10 | The provider carries the **primary safety responsibility** across the whole service lifecycle — algorithm-mechanism review, science-and-technology ethics review, content management, network and data security, risk plans and emergency response — with staffing matched to service type, scale and user profile. It must hold capabilities in privacy and personal-information protection, **over-dependency risk warning, emotional-boundary guidance and mental-health protection**, and must **not** set replacing social interaction, controlling users' psychology or inducing dependency as a service goal. |
| 11 | Training-data duties: lawful source; cleansing and labelling per national rules; diversity with negative sampling and adversarial training; **safety assessment of synthetic data** used for training or capability optimisation; routine inspection and updating; leak prevention. |
| 12 | A service agreement with the user, and collection of **age, guardian or emergency contact** as necessary information. |
| 13 | Risk must be identified in-flight and handled: on extreme emotion, generate soothing content and encouragement to seek help; where a user faces or has suffered **major property loss, or states an intent of self-harm or suicide**, take intervention measures and contact the guardian or emergency contact promptly. |
| 14 | **No virtual relatives or virtual partners to minors**; for other anthropomorphic services to under-14s, parental or guardian consent is required. A **minor mode** must exist, with mode switching, periodic reality reminders and usage-time limits; guardians can receive risk alerts, see usage summaries, block specific characters and cap spending. Effective measures must be taken to identify minor users, with a route to appeal. |
| 15 | For elderly users: guidance on safe use, prominent risk warnings, and timely response to their enquiries and calls for help. |
| 16 | Interaction data: data-property rules applied, encryption and access control; **no disclosure to third parties** unless another law provides or the rights holder expressly consents; users must be offered options to **copy and delete** their interaction history; **interaction data that is sensitive personal information may not be used for model training** without a legal basis or the user's separate consent. |
| 17 | Processing personal information of under-14s requires guardian consent, plus a **compliance audit** of that processing, by the provider itself or through a professional institution, per national rules. |
| 18 | The AI-generated-content **labelling duty applies here too**, and users must be prompted that they are interacting with an AI service and not a natural person. Where over-dependency or addiction tendencies appear, reminders must be given prominently and dynamically, e.g. by pop-up; **every two hours of continuous use** the user must be reminded, in dialogue or pop-up, to mind the time. |
| 19–20 | Exit must be convenient — by window operation, voice control or keyword the user can demand termination and the service must stop promptly, without sustaining interaction to block it. Stopping the service requires prior notice, or a public announcement where prior notice is impossible. |
| 21 | Accessible channels for user appeals and public complaints and reports, with a defined process and response deadline. |
| 22 | A **security assessment** is mandatory and the report goes to the provincial-level CAC department (which shares it with other departments) where: the service or a new related function goes online; new technology or application causes a material change; **registered users exceed 1,000,000 or monthly active users exceed 100,000**; risks may affect national security or public interest; or on other prescribed grounds. CAC departments at provincial level or above may order an assessment. |
| 23 | Assessment content: safeguards, training-data handling, identification and intervention for users in extreme situations, user scale, usage duration and age profile, minor and elderly protections, complaint handling, and remediation of risks reported by regulators. |
| 24–25 | On major risk the provider must restrict functions or suspend service and keep records; **app stores and distribution platforms** must verify the assessment and filing status before listing, and must refuse, warn, suspend or remove non-compliant apps. |
| 26 | Algorithm **filing, amendment and cancellation** are handled under the Provisions on the Administration of Algorithmic Recommendations; CAC departments run an **annual verification** of the filed materials. |
| 27–29 | Provincial CAC departments review assessment reports annually and may inspect on site; an AI **sandbox** service platform is being built with CAC and other departments; regulators may **summon the legal representative or principal officer** (约谈) over material risk or incidents. |
| 30 | Penalties: where other laws and administrative regulations provide, punishment follows them; otherwise warning and circulated criticism, an order to correct within a time limit, and possible suspension of new account registration or other services. **Refusal to correct or serious circumstances: an order to stop providing the service, and a fine of RMB 10,000–100,000. Where the harm touches citizens' life and health and damage results: a fine of RMB 100,000–200,000.** |
| 31 | Where the service touches **health care or finance**, the sector regulator's rules apply in addition. |

## Relationship to other laws

- **Legal basis named in Article 1** — Cybersecurity Law, Data Security Law, Personal Information Protection Law, and the Regulations on the Protection of Minors in Cyberspace (《未成年人网络保护条例》).
- **Article 18 turns the labelling regime onto this sector**: it points at the obligation created by the [Measures for Labelling AI-Generated Synthetic Content](ai-generated-content-labeling-measures.md) (effective 2025-09-01) and its mandatory standard GB 45438-2025.
- **Article 26 borrows the filing machinery** of the [Provisions on the Administration of Algorithmic Recommendations](algorithmic-recommendation-provisions.md) — the 2022 rule, not a new registry.
- **Fines are the smallest in this group of instruments**: RMB 200,000 at the ceiling here, against up to RMB 10,000,000 under the amended [Cybersecurity Law](cybersecurity-law.md) (in force 2026-01-01). The two bite on different things — this measure punishes the service conduct, the Cybersecurity Law punishes network-security failures — and a serious incident can engage both.
- **Against the EU**: there is no EU instrument addressed to AI companions as such. The nearest EU duty is the AI Act's transparency obligation for systems interacting directly with natural persons and for synthetic content ([Article 50](../eu/ai-act-transparency.md), applicable from 2026-08-02), which tells the user they are talking to a machine; it does not restrict virtual intimacy, cap usage time, or regulate dependency.

## Why this matters for the comparison

China now regulates the **emotional effect of a model on a named user** — dependency, manipulation, minors' access to virtual intimacy, the fate of chat logs — while the EU's comparable rule regulates **disclosure**. Both sides impose an information duty on the provider; only one of them makes the provider's persuasive influence over the user a compliance object.
