# Interim Measures for the Administration of Generative Artificial Intelligence Services

English · [中文](generative-ai-services-measures-zh.md)

> 生成式人工智能服务管理暂行办法 — CAC with six other departments, **Order No. 15**: adopted at the CAC's 12th executive meeting of 2023 on 23 May 2023, signed and promulgated **10 July 2023**, in force **15 August 2023**. Five chapters, twenty-four articles. China's first national-level rule addressed to generative AI services, and still the general statute that the later, narrower rules hang from.

## In one line

Whoever offers a generative AI service to the public inside mainland China is a **content producer** under Chinese law: the training data must be lawful, the output must not contain listed categories of illegal content, unlawful output must be stopped at the generation stage and reported, services with public-opinion or social-mobilisation capacity need a security assessment plus an algorithm filing — and the measure itself sets **no fine ceiling of its own**.

## Who and what it governs

- **Scope (Article 2)** — using generative AI technology to provide the public inside mainland China with services that generate **text, images, audio, video** and similar content. Where other rules govern generative AI used for publishing, film and television, or literary and artistic creation, those rules prevail.
- **Excluded (Article 2, third paragraph)** — industry associations, enterprises, education, science and research, public cultural institutions and other professional bodies that **research or apply** the technology **without offering a generative AI service to the domestic public** fall outside the measure.
- **Providers include API sellers (Article 22)** — a "provider" is any organisation or individual offering the service **including by way of a programmable interface**. A model exposed through an API is in scope even if the operator never sees an end user.
- **Users are defined separately (Article 22)** — organisations and individuals using the service to generate content; several duties run against them through the provider's contract.

## Key provisions (the ones that change behaviour)

| Article | What it requires |
|---|---|
| 3 | The state pursues **development and security in tandem**, encouraging innovation while governing generative AI services with **包容审慎** (inclusive, prudent) and **分类分级** (classified and tiered) regulation. |
| 4 | Five obligations on providing and using the service: uphold socialist values and generate none of the listed prohibited content; take effective measures against **discrimination on ethnicity, belief, country, region, gender, age, occupation or health** in algorithm design, training-data selection, model generation and optimisation, and service provision; respect intellectual property and commercial ethics, and do not use algorithm, data or platform advantage to monopolise or compete unfairly; respect others' lawful rights — no harm to physical or mental health, no infringement of **portrait, reputation, honour, privacy or personal-information rights**; and, matched to the service type, raise **transparency** and the **accuracy and reliability** of generated content. |
| 5–6 | Encouragement, not obligation: applications across industries, cooperative work on data and risk, **independent innovation in algorithms, frameworks, chips and supporting software**, international exchange and participation in international rule-making, training-data and compute infrastructure, and **orderly tiered opening of public data** to widen high-quality public training corpora. |
| 7 | Pre-training and optimisation training must: use data and base models of **lawful origin**; not infringe intellectual property; obtain **individual consent** or another lawful basis where personal information is involved; take effective measures to raise training-data quality and its **truthfulness, accuracy, objectivity and diversity**; and meet the Cybersecurity Law, Data Security Law, Personal Information Protection Law and other regulator requirements. |
| 8 | Where data annotation is done in the course of R&D, the provider must set **clear, specific and operable annotation rules**, run **quality assessment with sample verification** of annotated content, and train and supervise the annotators. |
| 9 | The provider bears **the responsibility of a network information content producer** and its network-security duties; where personal information is involved it bears **personal-information handler** responsibility. A **service agreement** with each registered user sets out rights and obligations. |
| 10 | State **who the service is for, in what settings, for what purposes**, and publish it; guide users toward scientific, rational and lawful use; take effective measures to **prevent minor users from over-dependency on or addiction to** the service. |
| 11 | Protect users' **input information and usage records**: do not collect personal information that is not necessary, do not unlawfully retain input or records that identify the user, do not unlawfully provide them to others; and handle requests to **access, copy, correct, supplement or delete** personal information promptly. |
| 12 | Label generated images, video and similar content **in accordance with the Provisions on the Administration of Deep Synthesis** — the labelling duty arrives by cross-reference, not by a new standard here. |
| 13 | Provide a **safe, stable and continuous** service so users can rely on it. |
| 14 | On discovering illegal content: promptly **stop generating and stop transmitting it, eliminate it**, remediate through **optimised model training**, and **report to the competent authorities**. On discovering a user committing unlawful acts through the service: warn, restrict functions, suspend or terminate service to that user by law and by contract, keep records, and report. |
| 15, 18 | Build complaint and reporting mechanisms with convenient entry points, published process and response deadlines; users may complain to the competent authorities about a service that breaches the law or these measures. |
| 16 | CAC, development-and-reform, education, science and technology, industry-and-information-technology, public security, broadcasting and press-and-publication departments regulate within their remit; competent departments are directed to develop **classified and tiered regulatory rules or guidance** suited to the technology. |
| 17 | A service with **public-opinion attributes or social-mobilisation capacity** must carry out a **security assessment** under national rules **and** perform algorithm **filing, amendment and cancellation** under the *Provisions on the Administration of Algorithmic Recommendations*. |
| 19 | Providers must cooperate with supervision and, on request, **explain the source, scale, type and annotation rules of training data and the mechanism of the algorithm**, supplying necessary technical and data support. Institutions and personnel taking part in assessments must keep state secrets, trade secrets, personal privacy and personal information confidential. |
| 20 | Where generative AI services are supplied **from outside mainland China into it** and do not meet Chinese law or these measures, the national cyberspace administration **notifies the relevant bodies to take technical and other necessary measures** — the statutory hook for blocking at the infrastructure layer. |
| 21 | **Penalties.** Where the Cybersecurity Law, Data Security Law, Personal Information Protection Law, Science and Technology Progress Law and other laws provide, those provisions are applied; **where they do not**, the competent departments give a **warning or circulated criticism and order correction within a time limit**, and on refusal to correct or in serious cases **order suspension of the relevant service**. Public-security and criminal liability follow where conduct amounts to those offences. |
| 23 | Where laws or administrative regulations require an administrative licence to provide the service, obtain it; foreign investment in generative AI services must comply with the foreign-investment rules. |

## What the penalty article does *not* do

Article 21 sets **no independent fine amount**. It routes punishment into the general laws — which is where the money actually is: the Personal Information Protection Law reaches **RMB 50 million or 5% of prior-year turnover** for serious violations ([Article 66](personal-information-protection-law.md)), and the amended Cybersecurity Law, in force 2026-01-01, reaches **RMB 10 million** with up to RMB 1 million on responsible individuals — on **two** separate triggers: Article 61 for network-security failures and Article 69 for prohibited information, both transcribed in [the Cybersecurity Law card](cybersecurity-law.md) — and leaves the measure's own sanction to be the **operational** one: warning, circulated criticism, order to correct, order to suspend the service. This is the answer to the question left open in [`../compare/01-ai-labeling.md`](../compare/01-ai-labeling.md): the generative-AI measures themselves carry no fine ceiling.

## Relationship to other laws

- **Named as its legal basis (Article 7(5), Article 21)** — Cybersecurity Law, Data Security Law, Personal Information Protection Law, and the Science and Technology Progress Law.
- **Article 12 imports the deep-synthesis labelling duty**, and Article 17 imports the **filing machinery** of the [Provisions on the Administration of Algorithmic Recommendations](algorithmic-recommendation-provisions.md). The 2025 [Measures for Labelling AI-Generated Synthetic Content](ai-generated-content-labeling-measures.md) later displaced the labelling detail; the [Anthropomorphic Interaction Services Measures](ai-anthropomorphic-interaction-measures.md) of 2026 add a companion-service layer on top.
- **The [Provisions on the Administration of Deep Synthesis](deep-synthesis-provisions.md)** (Order No. 12, effective 2023-01-10) remain in force alongside and are where the labelling duty of Article 12 actually comes from.
- **Against the EU**: the EU's comparable layer is the AI Act's **general-purpose AI model** regime, whose obligations start to apply **2025-08-02** — a model-side chapter (documentation, training-data summaries, copyright policy, systemic-risk duties) rather than a service-side licence. China regulates the **service offered to the public** and reaches the model through it; the AI Act regulates the model and reaches the service through its deployer rules. See [`../eu/ai-act.md`](../eu/ai-act.md).

## Why this matters for the comparison

This measure is why "China has no AI law" is misleading: it is not a statute, but from 15 August 2023 it has been the general obligation set for anyone publishing a generative service in China, and every narrower rule since (labelling, ethical review, anthropomorphic services) has been written to plug into it rather than to replace it. The EU's general statute came earlier in calendar terms but applies its model duties later than China's service duties took effect.
