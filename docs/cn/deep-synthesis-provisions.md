# Provisions on the Administration of Deep Synthesis of Internet Information Services

> 互联网信息服务深度合成管理规定 — CAC, MIIT and MPS **Order No. 12**: adopted at the CAC's 21st executive meeting of 2022 on 3 November 2022, signed and promulgated **25 November 2022**, in force **10 January 2023**. Five chapters, twenty-five articles. China's first binding rule on synthetic media, and still the origin of the labelling duty that the 2025 measures later gave technical detail to.

## In one line

Anyone offering deep-synthesis services to the public in China must verify users' real identity, review **both the input and the output**, attach a label to every synthesised item and a **prominent** label where the result could make the public mistake it for reality, never let anyone strip those labels, and — if the service has public-opinion attributes or social-mobilisation capacity — file the algorithm under the recommendation-algorithm rules. **The provisions set no fine of their own.**

## Who and what it governs

- **Scope (Article 2)** — applying deep-synthesis technology to provide internet information services inside mainland China. Where other laws or administrative regulations provide otherwise, those apply.
- **Deep-synthesis technology (Article 23)** — using generative and synthesising algorithms such as deep learning and virtual reality to produce text, images, audio, video or virtual scenes; the article lists five families, including text generation and style transfer, text-to-speech and voice conversion, music and scene-sound generation, **face generation, face swap, attribute editing, face and pose manipulation**, and image generation, enhancement and restoration. "Immersive simulated scenes" are defined as highly realistic virtual scenes generated or edited by the technology that participants can experience or interact with.
- **Two duty-holders** — the **service provider** (深度合成服务提供者) and the **technical supporter** (技术支持者). Article 19 makes technical supporters file on the same terms as providers; Articles 14, 15 and 21 place duties on both.
- **Regulators (Article 3)** — CAC coordinates nationally and locally; the telecom authority (MIIT) and public security act within their remit.

## Key provisions (the ones that change behaviour)

| Article | What it requires |
|---|---|
| 6 | No organisation or individual may use deep-synthesis services to make, copy, publish or spread information banned by law, or to endanger national security and interests, damage the state's image, infringe the public interest, disrupt economic and social order or violate others' lawful rights. **Providers and users must not use the service to produce or spread false news information**; republishing news made with deep synthesis must draw on news released by licensed internet news information source units. |
| 7–8 | The provider bears **primary responsibility for information security**, with management systems covering user registration, **algorithm-mechanism review, science-and-technology ethics review**, publication review, data security, personal-information protection, **telecom-fraud prevention** and emergency response, backed by safe and controllable technical measures. It must **publish its management rules and platform pledges**, complete service agreements, and remind technical supporters and users **prominently** of their information-security obligations. |
| 9 | Real-identity verification of users by **mobile phone number, ID-card number, unified social credit code, or the national public service for network identity authentication**; no information-publishing service may be provided to a user who has not been verified. |
| 10 | Review **users' input data and the synthesised result**, by technical or manual means. Maintain a **feature library for identifying illegal and harmful information**, with entry standards, rules and procedures, and keep network logs. On finding illegal or harmful information: dispose of it by law, keep records, **report promptly to the cyberspace administration and other competent departments**, and act against the user by law and contract — **warning, function restriction, service suspension or account closure**. |
| 11 | Build a **rumour-response mechanism**: on discovering false information made, copied, published or spread through the service, take rebutting measures promptly, keep records and report. |
| 12 | Provide convenient **user appeals and public complaints and reports**, publish the process and response deadlines, and handle and feed back in time. |
| 13 | **App stores and other distribution platforms** must carry out listing review, daily management and emergency response, **verify** a deep-synthesis app's **security assessment and filing**, and on breach refuse listing, warn, suspend or remove it. |
| 14 | Providers and technical supporters must manage training data and protect its security; **where training data contains personal information, the personal-information rules apply**. Where they offer **editing of biometric information such as faces and voices**, they must **prompt the user to inform the person being edited and obtain that person's separate consent**. |
| 15 | Regularly **review, assess and verify the mechanism of the generating and synthesising algorithms**. Where a model, template or other tool can (i) **generate or edit biometric information such as faces and voices**, or (ii) generate or edit **non-biometric special objects or scenes that may touch national security, the state's image, national interests or the public interest**, the actor must carry out a **security assessment**, itself or through a professional institution. |
| 16 | For information generated or edited with the service, the provider must **add a label by technical means that does not interfere with the user's use**, and retain log information as required by law and national rules. |
| 17 | Where the service **may cause the public to confuse or misidentify** the content, a **prominent label must be placed in a reasonable position or area of the content**, signalling that deep synthesis is involved. The listed services are: (i) simulated-person text generation or editing such as **intelligent dialogue and intelligent writing**; (ii) **synthesised or imitated voice**, or editing that markedly changes a person's identity features; (iii) **face generation, face replacement, face manipulation and pose manipulation**, or other image/video generation or editing that markedly changes identity features; (iv) **immersive simulated scenes**; (v) other services with generation or marked-editing functions. For deep-synthesis services outside those categories the provider must **supply a prominent-labelling function and prompt the user that it can be applied**. |
| 18 | **No organisation or individual may use technical means to delete, tamper with or conceal** the deep-synthesis labels required by Articles 16 and 17. |
| 19–20 | Providers of services **with public-opinion attributes or social-mobilisation capacity** must perform **filing, amendment and cancellation under the *Provisions on the Administration of Algorithmic Recommendations***; technical supporters do the same by reference. Those who have filed must **display their filing number in a prominent position** on the website or app and provide a public-information link. Developing and launching **new products, applications or functions** with those attributes requires a **security assessment** under national rules. |
| 21 | CAC, the telecom authority and public security inspect within their remit; providers and technical supporters must cooperate and supply necessary technical and data support. Where a **material information-security risk** is found, the authorities may require **suspension of information updates, of new account registration, or of other related services**, with rectification to remove the hazard. |
| 22 | **Penalties.** Violations are punished **under the relevant laws and administrative regulations**, with **heavier punishment where serious consequences result**; conduct amounting to a public-security breach is punished by the public security organs, and criminal liability follows where a crime is committed. |
| 24 | Providers and technical supporters engaged in internet publishing, network cultural activities or network audiovisual programme services must **also** meet the rules of the press-and-publication, culture-and-tourism and broadcasting authorities. |

## The labelling core, and what came after it

Articles 16, 17 and 18 are where China's AI-content labelling regime began: a **technical label that must not get in the user's way**, a **prominent label where confusion is possible**, and a **prohibition on removing either**. That three-part structure is the ancestor of the 2025 [Measures for Labelling AI-Generated Synthetic Content](ai-generated-content-labeling-measures.md) and its mandatory standard GB 45438-2025, which supply the format detail the provisions themselves leave open; the 2026 [Anthropomorphic Interaction Services Measures](ai-anthropomorphic-interaction-measures.md) Article 18 likewise points back at the labelling duty rather than replacing it. Both earlier instruments remain in force — reading the three as a stack rather than a replacement is this repository's characterisation, not a statement in the texts. — **C**

## Relationship to other laws

- **Legal basis named in Article 1** — Cybersecurity Law, Data Security Law, Personal Information Protection Law and the *Measures for the Administration of Internet Information Services*.
- **Article 19 imports the filing machinery** of the [Provisions on the Administration of Algorithmic Recommendations](algorithmic-recommendation-provisions.md); the same route reappears in Article 17 of the [generative-AI measures](generative-ai-services-measures.md) and Article 26 of the [anthropomorphic-services measures](ai-anthropomorphic-interaction-measures.md).
- **Article 14's separate-consent rule for faces and voices** is the operative portrait and voice protection in this area, sitting alongside the Personal Information Protection Law's sensitive-information rules.
- **Against the EU**: the AI Act's transparency clause covers synthetic content (machine-readable marking) and **deepfakes** (disclosure), applicable from 2026-08-02 — see [`../eu/ai-act-transparency.md`](../eu/ai-act-transparency.md). The EU duty is to disclose; these provisions additionally require **identity verification of the person doing the synthesising** (Article 9), **consent from the person being synthesised** (Article 14), **review of inputs as well as outputs** (Article 10), and a **rumour-response mechanism** (Article 11), and they bind **app stores** to gate the distribution channel (Article 13).

## Why this matters for the comparison

These provisions, not the AI Act and not the 2025 labelling measures, are where China's synthetic-media regime was set out — a year and a half before the EU's transparency duties began to apply. They also show the method this repository describes as China's: regulate a **service and its distribution chain** (provider, technical supporter, app store, user), reach the underlying model only through security assessment and algorithm review, and lean on the general laws for punishment.
