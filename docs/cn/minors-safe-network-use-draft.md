# Regulation on Ensuring Minors' Safe and Healthy Use of the Internet (consultation draft)

> 《国务院关于保障未成年人健康安全使用网络的规定（征求意见稿）》 — put out for public comment by the Cyberspace Administration of China on **18 September 2026**; comments close **17 October 2026**. Twenty-five articles. **Not adopted: this is a consultation draft** and is cited here as a proposal only. It is drafted for issue by the State Council as an administrative regulation, which would make it the first State Council-level instrument in this repository's China set. `published` and `effective` are empty by design: there is no instrument yet, and the draft's own Article 25 leaves the entry-into-force date blank (自2026年月日起施行). The English rendering here is ours; no official English text was found. ⚠

## In one line

China is proposing to write minors' online protection into a State Council regulation with its own penalty ladder: services must not hand a minor **stranger social networking**, **virtual relatives or virtual partners**, addiction-inducing services or livestream hosting; an under-16 may reach social networking, games, video and **AI services that may affect their cognition** only inside a **minor mode**; and platforms must file that mode with the regulator and report on it every year.

## What this document is — and what it is not

It is a **consultation draft**, not law. It carries no order number, no promulgating authority's seal and no entry-into-force date, and the CAC's notice asks the public for comments on it. Anything below may change or be dropped before adoption, and **no obligation here is in force**. Read it as evidence of the direction of Chinese regulation, and as the instrument that closes the reference the [Anthropomorphic Interaction Services Measures](ai-anthropomorphic-interaction-measures.md) already made to it. ⚠

Two structural points distinguish it from the other China cards. **First, it is drafted as an administrative regulation (行政法规) to be issued by the State Council**, not as a departmental measure — the instruments carded so far are CAC orders or joint departmental measures. **Second, it is drafted to carry its own penalties** rather than deferring entirely to other laws, in three bands keyed to which article was breached (Articles 18–20).

The drafting note published with it frames the exercise as 小快灵 — "small, quick, nimble" — legislation with Chinese characteristics for minors' online protection, and states that the draft runs to **twenty-five articles**.

## Key provisions

| Art. | What it requires |
|---|---|
| 1 | Purpose and legal basis: the **Protection of Minors Law**, the **Cybersecurity Law** and the **Personal Information Protection Law**. |
| 2 | Scope: providing online services **to minors inside China**; also developing or manufacturing **smart terminal products**, and running an **app distribution platform** for smart-terminal users. |
| 3 | The **best-interests-of-the-minor principle**: assess the potential effect on minors, set the audience and the service mode accordingly, and provide minor mode per national rules and standards. The state encourages a domestic **minors-only smart terminal** industry. |
| **4** | **Five prohibitions.** No minor may be given: (i) **stranger social networking** — except to a minor aged 16 or over who lives mainly on their own labour income; (ii) **virtual relatives, virtual partners or similar virtual-intimacy services**; (iii) services that induce addiction or otherwise endanger a minor's wellbeing; (iv) **livestream hosting** to an under-16; (v) anything else barred by law or national rules. |
| **5** | **Four service types an under-16 may be given only through minor mode**: audio/video, livestreaming other than hosting, and online games; **social networking** — microblogs, forums, groups, friend circles, direct messages, comment threads; **AI services that may affect a minor's cognition**; and any other service with a material effect on minors. |
| 6 | Minor-**identification** duty, using legal identity documents, the state network-identity service, distributed digital identity, non-storage one-off face verification and behavioural recognition. SIM cards for minors lacking full civil capacity are issued through their legal guardian. |
| 7 | Content management; **in minor mode no harmful content may be shown**; where a minor is in an extreme situation such as self-harm or suicide, the provider must intervene and contact the guardian or emergency contact. |
| 8 | Compliance systems — algorithm review, science-and-technology ethics review, content management, network and data security — and staff matched to the service. **No algorithmic model may be set up to induce a minor user's emotional dependency, addiction or excessive spending.** |
| 9 | Once a user is verified or identified as a minor, the Article 4 services must be withheld and the Article 5 services switched into minor mode; an appeal channel must be provided, and measures lifted after verification. |
| 10 | **Smart terminals**: minor-mode linkage, one-tap switching, exit verification, anti-bypass, and time-of-day, duration, function and content management. **App stores**: listing review, age-appropriateness assessment, and limiting download or installation of apps outside their applicable age range. Both must follow Articles 4 and 5 as well. |
| 11 | Minor mode must meet national rules and standards and must detect harmful content, protect minors' personal information, prevent addiction and help guardians. The CAC with other departments builds the supporting **technical standards**. |
| 12 | **Filing registry**: terminal makers and service providers file their minor-mode configuration with the **provincial** CAC; providers with **huge numbers of minor users or significant influence** file with the **national** CAC through the province and report on the build and use of minor mode; the CAC conducts an **annual verification**. Also telecom type-approval testing of terminal minor mode, and the press-and-publication authority's games-addiction work. |
| 13–17 | Education departments and schools (13, 16), public security (14), mass organisations (15), guardians (17). |
| **18** | **Penalties on the core duties** (Articles 4, 5, 6(1), 7(3), 9(1), 11(1)): order to correct, warning, confiscation of illegal gains; where gains exceed **RMB 1,000,000**, a fine of **1 to 10 times the gains**; otherwise **RMB 100,000–1,000,000**; responsible officers **RMB 10,000–100,000**. Refusal to correct or serious circumstances: suspension of the business, closure of the site or app, revocation of the licence or business licence. |
| 19 | Breach of the minor-mode switching duty (Article 9(2)): **RMB 50,000–500,000**. |
| 20 | Breach of the smart-terminal and app-store duties (Articles 10, 12(2)): **RMB 100,000–1,000,000**. |
| 21 | Otherwise the Protection of Minors Law, Cybersecurity Law, PIPL, the **Regulations on the Protection of Minors in Cyberspace** and the Telecom Regulations apply; civil, public-security and criminal liability are unaffected. |
| 24 | The CAC may adjust the Article 4 and Article 5 service lists over time; offering minor mode to a minor **aged 16 or over** is encouraged. |
| 25 | **Entry into force is left blank** — the draft reads 自2026年月日起施行, a 2026 date not yet filled in. |

## Relationship to other instruments

- **It generalises what the anthropomorphic-interaction measures already did for one sector.** Article 14 of the [Interim Measures for AI Anthropomorphic Interaction Services](ai-anthropomorphic-interaction-measures.md) (in force 2026-07-15) bans virtual relatives and partners to minors **for anthropomorphic services**; Article 4(2) of this draft bans them for **any** network service offered to a minor, and Article 4(1) adds stranger social networking on top — the drafting note gives that pairing (陌生人网络社交服务、虚拟亲属和虚拟伴侣等虚拟亲密关系服务) as the substance of Article 4.
- **Named as the legal basis or the fallback**: Article 1 cites the [Cybersecurity Law](cybersecurity-law.md), the [Personal Information Protection Law](personal-information-protection-law.md) and the Protection of Minors Law; Article 21 routes anything not covered by Articles 18–20 to those laws plus the **Regulations on the Protection of Minors in Cyberspace** and the Telecom Regulations.
- **The AI hook is Article 5(3)**: "AI services that may affect a minor's cognition" is written into the list of services an under-16 may use **only through minor mode**. No earlier Chinese instrument in this set names AI services as a category that triggers a mode switch; Order No. 21 regulated the AI service itself, this draft regulates minors' **access** to it.
- **Against the EU**: the EU does not have a minors' instrument of this kind either. The comparable duties are spread across three texts: [DSA Article 28](../eu/dsa-dma.md) (platforms accessible to minors must ensure a high level of privacy, safety and security, and must not show minors profiling-based advertising), [AI Act Article 5(1)(b)](../eu/ai-act.md) (prohibited practice: exploiting vulnerabilities **due to age** with the objective or effect of materially distorting behaviour causing significant harm) and Article 50's disclosure duty for systems interacting with people ([AI Act transparency duties](../eu/ai-act-transparency.md)). The EU rule that most nearly matches Article 4(2) of this draft is a **prohibition** aimed at the system's design; the Chinese draft instead **withholds named service types from minors** and builds a mode, a filing registry and a penalty ladder around the withholding. This comparison is this repository's reading. — **C**
- **Enforcement design worth watching**: the penalty for the core duties is keyed to **illegal gains** at the top band (1–10×), the same multiplier structure as the [Cybersecurity Law](cybersecurity-law.md)'s amended fines but on a revenue base rather than a fixed ceiling. Whether the gain base survives adoption is unresolved. ⚠

## Why this matters for the comparison

Both systems now treat minors as a category that changes what a service may do, but they pull in different directions. The EU's DSA pulls the **platform's design** (no profiling-based ads to minors; a high level of privacy, safety and security) and the AI Act pulls **prohibited exploitation of age-based vulnerability**; both leave the service available. China's draft instead **removes named service types** from minors altogether — stranger social networking, virtual intimacy, livestream hosting under 16 — and gates a second list (social networking, games, video, **cognitive-affecting AI**) behind a mode that the provider must **file with the regulator and report on annually**. It is the mode-plus-registry pattern, not the risk-assessment pattern, applied to minors' access as such.
