# Provisions on the Security Assessment of Internet Information Services with Public-Opinion Attributes or Social-Mobilisation Capacity

English · [中文](social-mobilisation-security-assessment-provisions-zh.md)

> 具有舆论属性**或**社会动员能力的互联网信息服务安全评估规定 — issued jointly by the CAC and the Ministry of Public Security on **15 November 2018**, in force **30 November 2018**. Seventeen articles, **no order number**: the CAC's own account calls it 政策性文件 (a policy document), outside the administrative-permission system and setting **no penalty of its own**. Its importance in this repository is specific: **Article 2 is the only official text that delimits the phrase "public-opinion attributes or social-mobilisation capacity"**, which is the trigger for filing under the algorithmic-recommendation provisions (Art. 24), for assessment under the deep-synthesis provisions (Arts. 19–20), Article 17 of the generative-AI measures and Article 22 of the anthropomorphic-services measures. The English rendering here is ours; no official English text was found. ⚠

## In one line

If your service can shape public opinion or rally people — a forum, a blog, a Weibo, a chatroom, a group chat, an official account, short video, livestream, information sharing or a mini-program — you must **assess your own security before launch**, file the report with the cyberspace and public-security authorities through the national platform, and accept review; refuse, and the state **publishes a risk warning about you** rather than fining you.

## The delimitation every other instrument defers to this one

- **Article 2 — the definition.** The services covered are (i) running, or adding the function of, 论坛、博客、微博客、聊天室、通讯群组、公众账号、短视频、网络直播、信息分享、小程序 — forums, blogs, microblogs, chatrooms, communications groups, public accounts, short video, livestream, information sharing and mini-programs; and (ii) any other internet information service that **provides a channel for public opinion to be expressed, or has the capacity to mobilise the public to engage in a particular activity**.
- **Article 3 — the five occasions that oblige a self-assessment**, for which the provider is responsible for the result: launching such a service or adding the relevant function; using new technology or a new application that materially changes the service's functional attributes, technical implementation or underlying resources so that its public-opinion or mobilisation capacity changes materially; a **significant increase in user numbers** with the same effect; **spread of illegal or harmful information** showing that existing measures cannot control the risk; and any other case notified **in writing** by a cyberspace or public-security authority at or above prefecture level.
- Note the internal inconsistency worth recording: the **title uses 或** while the body of Articles 2 and 3 alternates between 或 and 或者. Nothing turns on it, but a citation should follow the text it quotes.

## Key provisions

| Art. | What it requires |
|---|---|
| 1 | Purpose and legal basis: the **Cybersecurity Law**, the *Measures for the Administration of Internet Information Services* and the *Measures for the Administration of Security Protection of Computer Information Networks with International Connections*. |
| **2** | Defines the services with public-opinion attributes or social-mobilisation capacity — the ten named categories plus the two-limb catch-all above. |
| **3** | The five triggers for self-assessment; **the provider is answerable for the assessment result**. |
| 4 | The assessment may be done **by the provider itself or entrusted to a third-party assessment institution**. |
| 5 | What must be assessed: legality of the service and of the new technology or application, effectiveness of the security measures required by laws, regulations, rules and standards, and risk control — with **eight named focal points**: an adequate security manager, content-review staff or a security body; **verification of users' real identity and retention of registration information**; **retention of logs** — account, operation time, operation type, source and target network address, source port, **client hardware characteristics** — and of published-information records; measures against illegal or harmful information in account and group names, nicknames, profiles, notes and labels, in posting, reposting, commenting and group functions, with records kept; personal-information protection and the **technical measures against spread of illegal or harmful information and against loss of control of the mobilisation function**; a complaints and reports system with published channels and timely handling; a working mechanism to give technical and data support and assistance to the cyberspace authorities; and the same for public-security and state-security organs in maintaining national security and investigating crime. |
| 6 | Hazards found must be remedied until eliminated; a **security assessment report** conforming to laws, regulations, rules and standards must be produced, covering the service's function, scope, hardware and software, deployment location and licences obtained; the security management and technical measures implemented and their effect; the assessment conclusion; and anything else that should be stated. |
| **7** | **Where and when to file.** The report goes **through the national internet security management service platform** (全国互联网安全管理服务平台) to the cyberspace and public-security authorities **at or above prefecture level where the provider is located**. For the Article 3(i)–(ii) cases, **before** the service, new technology or application goes online or the function is added; for Article 3(iii)–(v), **within 30 working days** of the circumstance arising. |
| 8 | Prefecture-level and higher authorities conduct a **written review**; if the report is incomplete or the method plainly inappropriate they **order a re-assessment within a set period**, and if it is unclear they may order supplementary explanation. |
| 9 | On the strength of the written review, **on-site inspection** may follow; in principle the two authorities inspect **jointly**, and must not disturb normal business. |
| 10 | For services with material risk that may affect national security, public order or the public interest, **provincial-level and higher** authorities organise an **expert review panel**, and may inspect jointly with local departments. |
| 11–12 | Inspections follow the existing laws and regulations; the authorities run monitoring and management systems and, on finding an unassessed service, **notify the provider to carry out the assessment**. |
| **13** | **The consequence of refusal.** Where a provider **refuses** to assess, the authorities must use the national platform to **warn the public that the service carries security risks**, and inspect it under their respective duties, handling any illegal act according to law. |
| 14 | The cyberspace authority coordinates; the public-security authority **reports its assessment work to the cyberspace authority periodically**. |
| 15 | State secrets, trade secrets and personal information learned in this work must be kept strictly confidential — no disclosure, sale or unlawful provision. |
| 16 | For new technologies and applications in **internet news information services**, assessment follows the separate *Provisions on the Administration of Security Assessment of New Technologies and Applications in Internet News Information Services*. |
| 17 | In force from **30 November 2018**. |

## What it is not — and why that matters for the comparison

The CAC's own description of the instrument is explicit on three points: it is a **policy document** (政策性文件), it is **not an administrative-permission matter** (不属于行政许可事项), and it **creates no administrative penalty** (不设立行政处罚措施); for a service that goes online without assessment, the authorities publish a **security risk warning** on the national platform ([CAC, 网络传播杂志](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2019-03/20/c_1124259405.htm)). The mechanism is therefore **registration-plus-exposure**, not licensing: the state does not approve the service, it makes the provider write down how it discharges duties it already owes under the Cybersecurity Law, routes the report to two authorities, and names it publicly if it refuses. — **A** for the text, **B** for this characterisation (an official statement about the instrument, not the instrument itself)

Two consequences follow for the AI instruments that point here. **First**, the assessment these products must do is not the AI-specific security assessment the generative-AI measures' drafters were asked about — it is this 2018 obligation, keyed to the *kind of service*, and it predates all of them. **Second**, because this instrument sets no penalty, the enforcement teeth in the AI rules sit where the cards said they do: in the Cybersecurity Law's Chapter VI (Arts. 61–77) and in the operational sanctions of the newer measures, not in the assessment duty itself.

## Relationship to other instruments

- **Named in the referral chains of three AI instruments.** The [Deep Synthesis Provisions](deep-synthesis-provisions.md) Article 20 (new products, applications and functions "according to relevant national regulations"), the [Generative AI Services Measures](generative-ai-services-measures.md) Article 17, and the [Anthropomorphic Interaction Services Measures](ai-anthropomorphic-interaction-measures.md) Article 22 all condition their obligations on services with **public-opinion attributes or social-mobilisation capacity**; Article 2 of this instrument is where that phrase is defined. The same phrase drives **filing** under Article 24 of the [Algorithmic Recommendation Provisions](algorithmic-recommendation-provisions.md).
- **Legal basis (Article 1)** — the [Cybersecurity Law](cybersecurity-law.md) (real-identity, logs and incident duties the assessment checks against), plus the two administrative regulations named there.
- **Article 16 carves out** internet news information services, which follow their own new-technology assessment rules instead.
- **Against the EU**: there is no EU counterpart to a pre-launch self-assessment filing keyed to a service's capacity to move public opinion. The AI Act's conformity assessment is keyed to **risk classification of the system** and, for GPAI models, to technical documentation — see [`../eu/ai-act.md`](../eu/ai-act.md). The nearest structural analogy in EU data law is the GDPR's DPIA (Art. 35), which is **internal, not filed**, and triggered by high-risk processing rather than by audience reach. This comparison is this repository's reading. — **C**
