# AI liability and pending legislation in China

> China has **no dedicated AI law yet** and no AI-specific civil liability instrument. Harm from AI is handled by general civil law plus administrative rules — and comprehensive AI legislation is now an explicit item on the State Council's 2026 legislative plan.

## In one line

Liability today is **administrative-first**: regulators act through filing, assessment, takedown and fines; private claims fall back on the Civil Code. A comprehensive AI law is now formally on the legislative agenda.

## Comprehensive AI legislation — status

> **This is a status note, not a law card.** No AI law exists yet, so `published` and `effective` are empty by design: there is no instrument to date. The routes below are the ones currently in force.

- The **State Council 2026 Legislative Work Plan** (issued 11 May 2026) directs: *accelerate comprehensive legislation for the healthy development of artificial intelligence*, and improve legislation on AI's shared elements — data, computing power, algorithms, intellectual property, network security, supply chain security — and on key application scenarios.
- The framing has moved from "study" to **comprehensive legislation**, but no bill text has been published. Treat any date as speculative ⚠.

## Where liability currently sits

| Route | Instrument | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Civil — tort | Civil Code, tort liability chapter (effective 2021-01-01) | Fault-based liability, with special rules for specified situations. |
| Civil — product | Civil Code, product liability provisions | Applies to defective products; whether an AI-enabled service counts as a "product" is unsettled ⚠. |
| Statutory AI clause | Cybersecurity Law, new **Article 20** (amendment adopted 2025-10-28, in force 2026-01-01) | Supports basic AI research, training-data and computing infrastructure, AI ethics and risk monitoring, and the use of AI to raise network-security protection. A promotional clause — it creates no liability of its own. |
| Administrative | Interim Measures for Generative AI Services (effective 2023-08-15) | Filing, security assessment, content compliance; penalties handled under the Cybersecurity Law, Data Security Law, PIPL and similar. The amended Cybersecurity Law (in force 2026-01-01) now reaches up to **RMB 10 million** for the most serious network-security failures (Articles 61–69), with up to RMB 1 million on responsible individuals. |
| Administrative | Algorithmic Recommendation Provisions (Order No. 9) | Filing, security assessment, warnings, suspension of information updates, RMB 10,000–100,000 fines. |
| Ethics | Measures for Ethical Review of Science and Technology (trial) (effective 2023-12-01) | Ethics review as a pre-condition for certain research and applications. |

## What is not there yet

- No statutory definition of AI harm or of the liable party across the chain (developer, provider, deployer, user).
- No dedicated burden-of-proof rule for AI-related damage — the gap the withdrawn EU directive was meant to fill.
- No published draft, so the eventual scope (registration? liability allocation? model-level duties?) is unknown.
