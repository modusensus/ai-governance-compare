# Product liability and the withdrawn AI Liability Directive

> The EU's answer to "who pays when AI causes harm" is **not** a new AI-specific liability law. The 2022 AI Liability Directive was **withdrawn**; instead the revised Product Liability Directive folds software and AI into the existing product-liability framework.

## In one line

No AI-specific civil liability in the EU: the Commission dropped the AI Liability Directive and modernised product liability instead, so defective software and AI count as defective products.

## New Product Liability Directive — Directive (EU) 2024/2853

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 2022-09-28 | proposal published |
| 2024-10-23 | date the directive carries (OJ publication followed on 2024-11-18) |
| 2024-12-08 | entered into force (twentieth day after OJ publication) |
| **2026-12-09** | deadline for Member States to transpose into national law (Article 22) |

What it changes:

- **Software and AI systems count as products** — the old directive (85/374/EEC) assumed physical goods.
- **Disclosure duty** — a claimant can ask the court to order the defendant to disclose evidence; refusal can affect the outcome.
- **Rebuttable presumption** — where a product is technically or scientifically complex, a defect and its causal link may be presumed, easing the claimant's evidential burden.
- Applies to products placed on the market after the transposition date, so the practical effect arrives from **December 2026**.

## AI Liability Directive — withdrawn

- Proposed 28 September 2022 to ease the burden of proof in AI-related damage claims (presumptions, disclosure of evidence).
- The Commission **withdrew the proposal** in its 2025 Work Programme, adopted **11 February 2025**, and signalled no near-term replacement; a future Commission could revive the idea if PLD case law exposes a gap, but that would be a multi-year process.

## Practical state of play

- Until national transposition completes, liability sits with national tort law plus sector rules.
- After December 2026, a defective AI-enabled product can be pursued under product liability with a softer evidential burden.
- Pure economic loss and non-product AI harm remain the acknowledged gaps.
