# GDPR Chapter V — Transfers of personal data to third countries

> Articles 44–49 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679. One principle sits above everything: personal data may leave the EU **only if** one of the mechanisms applies — and it must keep its protection level as it travels.

## In one line

The EU runs a **country-level trust model plus standardised contracts**: if the destination has an adequacy decision, data flows; if not, you need safeguards such as standard contractual clauses, and only in narrow cases a derogation.

## The mechanisms

| Article | Mechanism | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **44** | General principle | Any transfer, including onward transfers, must maintain the level of protection. |
| **45** | **Adequacy decision** | The Commission finds a country, territory or sector provides an essentially equivalent level of protection. No further authorisation needed. |
| **46** | **Appropriate safeguards** | Standard contractual clauses (SCCs), binding corporate rules (BCRs, requiring Article 47 approval), approved codes of conduct, certification — plus enforceable data-subject rights and effective remedies. |
| **47** | Binding corporate rules | For intra-group transfers by multinationals. |
| **49** | **Derogations** | Explicit consent, necessity for a contract or its performance, important reasons of public interest, legal claims, vital interests, public registers — narrow and read restrictively. |

## Practical history worth knowing

- **Schrems II (2020)** invalidated the Privacy Shield and hardened transfer impact assessments; the SCCs were revised and reissued in 2021.
- **EU–US Data Privacy Framework (adequacy decision, July 2023)** restored a route for certified US recipients — and remains contested.

## Where the friction lands

- Adequacy is a **political, country-level** judgement, so it moves slowly and is vulnerable to litigation.
- SCCs are **standardised by the Commission**, which means the contract is largely non-negotiable.
- Transfers within a multinational group usually require BCRs — a heavy authorisation process.
