# Digital Services Act (DSA) and Digital Markets Act (DMA)

> Two regulations aimed at platforms. The **DSA** regulates how platforms handle content and risk; the **DMA** regulates how the largest platforms compete. Both take a size-based approach: the bigger you are, the more you owe.

## In one line

The DSA makes platforms accountable for illegal content, transparency and systemic risk; the DMA opens up gatekeepers — no self-preferencing, interoperability, and limits on combining personal data.

## DSA — Regulation (EU) 2022/2065

| When | What |
|---|---|
| 2022-11-16 | entered into force |
| 2023-08-25 | obligations apply to designated **VLOPs / VLOSEs** (very large online platforms and search engines) |
| 2024-02-17 | applies to **all other in-scope intermediary services** |

Core duties:

- **Notice and action**, statement of reasons for moderation, internal complaint handling.
- **Transparency of recommender systems** (Article 27) — the main options used must be explained.
- **Advertising**: no targeted advertising to minors (Article 28); no advertising based on sensitive data.
- **Systemic risk**: VLOPs must run risk assessments, adopt mitigation measures, and undergo independent audits (Articles 34–35), with a crisis-response mechanism (Article 36).
- **Researcher data access** (Article 40).
- **Penalties**: up to **6% of total worldwide annual turnover**.

## DMA — Regulation (EU) 2022/1925

| When | What |
|---|---|
| 2022-11-01 | entered into force |
| 2024-03-07 | obligations became applicable; **gatekeepers** designated by the Commission |

Gatekeeper obligations include:

- No self-preferencing of own services; no anti-steering restrictions.
- Allow sideloading and alternative app stores.
- Messaging interoperability.
- No combining personal data across services without consent.
- FRAND access for business users.
- **Penalties**: Article 30 — up to **10% of total worldwide turnover** in the preceding financial year; up to **20%** for repeated infringement.

## Overlap with the AI Act and the GDPR

- AI systems on platforms can trigger the AI Act's transparency duties, the DSA's content duties and the GDPR's data duties at once.
- The DMA's data-combination limits interact directly with GDPR consent.
