# Data Governance Act and Data Act

> The EU's "data strategy" pair. The **Data Governance Act** builds the plumbing for sharing data; the **Data Act** decides who gets to use the data that connected products generate. Together they are the non-personal counterpart to the GDPR.

## In one line

The DGA creates trusted intermediaries and rules for re-using protected public-sector data; the Data Act gives users of connected devices a right to the data those devices produce — and to share it with third parties.

## Data Governance Act — Regulation (EU) 2022/868

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 2022-06-23 | entered into force |
| **2023-09-24** | became applicable, after a 15-month transition |

What it does:

- opens up **re-use of protected public-sector data** (commercially confidential, personal, IP-protected) under conditions that preserve rights;
- creates a regime for **data intermediation services** — providers must register and may not use the data for their own purposes;
- recognises **data altruism** organisations through a voluntary registration;
- sets up the European Data Innovation Board.

## Data Act — Regulation (EU) 2023/2854

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 2024-01-11 | entered into force (twentieth day after the OJ publication of 22 December 2023 — not the 13 December signature date) |
| **2025-09-12** | became applicable |

What it does:

- gives **users of connected products** (IoT, vehicles, machinery) a right to access the data they generate, and to ask the data holder to share it with a third party;
- regulates **cloud switching** — removing obstacles to moving between providers;
- protects against **unfair contract terms** imposed on SMEs;
- allows **business-to-government** data sharing in exceptional public-interest situations.

## Why this pair matters for AI

- AI training data increasingly comes from connected products and public-sector holdings — exactly what these two regulations govern.
- Neither is about personal data alone; both sit alongside the GDPR rather than replacing it.
