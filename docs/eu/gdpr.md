# General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)

> Regulation (EU) 2016/679. Applicable since 25 May 2018. The reference model for personal-data law, and the yardstick China's PIPL is usually compared against.

## In one line

A regulation built on rights: it sets principles for processing, a closed list of lawful bases, a strong rights catalogue, heavy fines, and a dedicated cross-border transfer chapter.

## Who and what it governs

- **Article 3 (territorial scope)** — applies to processing in the EU, and to controllers or processors outside the EU where they offer goods or services to people in the EU or monitor their behaviour.
- Covers personal data of individuals in the EU, regardless of nationality.

## Key provisions

| Topic | Provision |
|---|---|
| Principles | **Article 5** — lawfulness, fairness, transparency; purpose limitation; data minimisation; accuracy; storage limitation; integrity and confidentiality; accountability. |
| Lawful bases | **Article 6** — consent; contract; legal obligation; vital interests; public task; **legitimate interests** (with a balancing test and a right to object). |
| Special categories | **Article 9** — racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs, trade-union membership, genetic and biometric data for identification, health, sex life or sexual orientation. Processing is prohibited unless an exception applies. |
| Rights | **Articles 12–22** — information, access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, objection, and rights on automated decision-making including profiling. |
| Breach notification | **Articles 33–34** — notify the supervisory authority within **72 hours**; notify individuals where the risk is high. |
| Impact assessment | **Article 35** — DPIA for high-risk processing. |
| Data protection officer | **Articles 37–39**. |
| Cross-border transfers | **Chapter V, Articles 44–49** — adequacy decision, appropriate safeguards (SCCs, BCRs), or derogations. |
| Penalties | **Article 83** — two tiers: up to **EUR 10 million or 2%** of total worldwide annual turnover, and up to **EUR 20 million or 4%** for the most serious breaches. |
| Regulators | National supervisory authorities plus the European Data Protection Board. |

## Relationship to other EU laws

- The AI Act layers AI-specific duties on top; the Data Act and Data Governance Act govern non-personal and mixed data; the ePrivacy Directive covers cookies and electronic communications.
