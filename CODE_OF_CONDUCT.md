# Code of Conduct

This repository compares how the EU and China regulate AI and data. What it is about is the
relations of production in the age of AI, data sovereignty, and the governance of algorithms.

This code is built on two things: the **labour theory of value**, and **resistance to alienation**.
It does not list protected identities. Section 1 says why.

---

## 0. A boundary: this code does not change the licence

Nothing here narrows the rights granted by MIT and CC-BY-SA-4.0.

| | Bound by this code |
|---|---|
| People who **use** this repository's content | No. The licence already grants it; we add no conditions |
| People who **participate** here (pull requests, reviews, discussion) | Yes. These are the rules of our own house |

Take these law cards and do what you like with them — the licence governs that, not us. This document
governs one thing only: **how we treat each other's labour.**

---

## 1. Labour theory of value: value comes from labour, so labour keeps its name

Class is not an attribute a person carries. It is a **relation** — who owns the means of production, who
sells their labour, who sets the terms, who bears the risk. An attribute can be respected; a relation can
only be changed. So this code governs conduct within that relation, not identity.

In practice, four checkable rules:

- **Name the person.** Every contribution is attributed where it appears. No ghostwriting, no "we" that
  absorbs an individual's work, no quietly rewriting someone's paragraph until it reads as yours.
- **Translation is labour.** A `-zh.md` file is not a draft and not a lesser artefact; it is credited as
  labour.
- **Invisible labour counts too.** Reviewing, fixing a dead link, checking an effective date — that is
  labour, and it says who did it.
- **Do not take over another person's pull request.** If it has stalled, ask; do not push over it.

We oppose large technology companies and capital entities taking community labour without payment and
turning it into a commercial barrier. That targets a practice, not any particular company.

---

## 2. Voice is allocated by evidence, not by identity

- Whether a change stands is decided by the source, the date and the text of the provision — **not by the
  contributor's seniority, institution, degree, language, past contributions or star count**.
- A **jurisdiction's rule may be called worse. A person may not be called worse for being subject to it.**
- If you find yourself explaining an error by who someone is rather than by what the text says, stop.

This holds in both directions: it protects the people whose positions you disagree with. We do not show
inclusion by applying labels, and we do not exclude by applying them either.

---

## 3. Technology must serve human liberation

This section is a **statement of the maintainers' position**, not a restriction on downstream use (see
Section 0).

- We welcome reasoned argument on technology for the public good, algorithmic fairness and workers'
  rights — especially argument that lands on a provision or a decided case.
- We **do not accept** contributions made to serve surveillance, the extraction of labour, or deepening
  social injustice. Maintainers reserve the right to decline such a contribution. That is "we will not
  take your pull request", not "you may not use this".
- We **do not pretend to be neutral.** A repository that compares two jurisdictions will offend someone.
  The way to offend is to quote the text, not to shout a slogan.

---

## 4. Solidarity: write down the real barriers

"Background does not matter" is an empty sentence unless it is spelled out. These are the barriers we know
about, and what we do about them.

| Barrier | How it actually bites | What this repository does |
|---|---|---|
| **Language** | English is the default; writing in a second language takes three times as long and reads as less confident | A Chinese version sits beside every card; translation is credited as labour, never treated as a rough draft |
| **Network** | Automated link checks cannot reach official sites that serve mainland China only (`.gov.cn` and others), so those links are excluded from CI | A source verifiable only from one network is **not** downgraded for that reason. State the environment you checked from; the maintainer judges on that basis |
| **Time** | Unpaid work has no service-level agreement | No response-time expectation on anyone, maintainers included. A stalled pull request is not an abandoned one — ask before acting |
| **Credentials** | Institutions have letterheads; individuals do not | Affiliation, degree and employer are not evidence. **An A-grade source cited by a student is worth exactly as much as the same source cited by a ministry** |

Whether you are an independent researcher, a student or an ordinary developer, if the source holds up,
there is a place for you here.

## 5. Unacceptable behaviour

- Removing or obscuring attribution; presenting another person's work as your own.
- Fabricating, mis-citing or laundering a source; passing off a summary page, a news report or a model's
  output as the primary text.
- Using a person's identity as a substitute for an argument about the text.
- Personal attacks, insults, belittling or mocking contributors.
- Harassment, stalking, threats, doxxing, or publishing private information.
- Sexualised language or imagery.
- Brigading, ragebait, coordinated inauthentic activity, or rank manipulation — including automated
  starring. GitHub's Acceptable Use Policies §4 treats that as abuse; so do we.

---

## 6. Enforcement: repair before removal

1. **Repair.** Where the harm is a wrong attribution, a bad citation or a misrepresented position, the
   first remedy is to put it right: restore the name, correct the record, reinstate withdrawn text, and
   note the correction in public.
2. **Warning.** A written notice naming the clause and the specific act. Not "you were hostile" — which
   sentence, in which thread.
3. **Restriction.** A limit on one channel or one thread, for a stated period.
4. **Removal.** Last, not first. A permanent ban is for conduct that continues after warning, or for harm
   that cannot be repaired — doxxing, threats, fabricated sources at scale.

Rules on the people applying this:

- A decision to restrict or remove cites the clause and the evidence. **Unexplained removal is itself a
  violation of this code.**
- Maintainers do not own contributors' labour. Merging a pull request does not transfer authorship, and
  closing one does not delete what it taught.
- A maintainer with a stake in the dispute — including the maintainer who wrote the disputed text — says
  so and asks someone else to decide.
- Disagreement with a maintainer's decision goes to a public issue, not a private message.

---

## 7. Scope

This applies in issues, pull requests, discussions, and in public statements made on behalf of the
project. It does not apply to how downstream users use this repository's content (see Section 0).
